    P2 · Visitor & tourism spending

    Out-of-town visitor spending, with multiplier

    P · Proxy, inputs assumed
    —
    Mountain bike tourism proxy; the inputs are assumptions. No visitor-spending study exists for pump tracks or skateparks. The benchmark is bike-park and trail-network spending ($94–$133 per visitor-day, Whistler, as cited in NW Arkansas studies; not re-checked here). The out-of-town share (1.5–4%) has no source. The spend values are scaled down from the benchmark by judgment. The 1.72× multiplier is a national outdoor-recreation figure (OIA, 2022) and may overstate a small local facility. Spending counts only new money from people assumed to travel because of the facility; local spending displaced from elsewhere is not netted out.

    Methodology notes

    P1

    Anchored to one study; scaling is assumed

    P1 = construction cost × annual surplus rate. Kemp (2025) is a travel-cost model of one 88,000 sq ft destination skatepark. Its surplus is driven by visits and travel distance, not by what the park cost, so tying the rate to construction cost is this tool’s own device. The 3.0–7.2% rates are set below the 7.9% baseline for smaller and more local facilities; how much below is a judgment call. Neighborhood facilities serve mostly short-distance users, who generate little travel-cost surplus.

    P2

    Proxy data; fraction and spend are assumed

    P2 = average daily users × out-of-town share × operating days × spend per visitor-day × multiplier. Operating days adjust for climate: 340+ in mild climates, about 300 in temperate, 260–299 in northern, 180–259 in cold ones. Spend by type is judgment, not a derived income adjustment. MTB households skew above $75K and skateboarding households sit nearer the national median (NSGA survey data), which is the reason for setting skatepark spend lower, but pump tracks serve many children and families, so adult MTB income is a weak basis for the higher pump-track values. Facility scale moves the spend values more than type does. The spend shown is direct spending before the multiplier; the result line also shows it after.

    ≠

    Not modeled

    Maintenance and operating cost, discounting, ramp-up, inflation, displacement of local spending, youth users (Kemp counts adults only), and health, safety and crime effects. Dollar year is not stated by Kemp, and the cost benchmarks are undated planning values.
